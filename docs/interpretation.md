# Chapter 5: interpretation

The dissertation primarily studies same-year measurement and nowcasting. Annual forecasting comparisons are supporting analyses with their own information boundaries. A useful same-year association does not establish that the same model can predict an unforeseen political event in advance.

Carry five qualifications into the conclusions:

- **Dependence:** repeated observations within countries and common calendar years matter for uncertainty.
- **Model selection:** selecting a favourable estimator changes how an unadjusted significance result should be read.
- **Construct validity:** disagreement between raters can reveal measurement instability, but spread alone is not accuracy.
- **Historical provenance:** retained inputs do not establish an exact Wikipedia revision for every original measurement.
- **Decision use:** a hypothetical review rule or penalty schedule does not demonstrate realised financial savings or investment returns.

The [bounded checks](reference/chapter5.md), [origin comparisons](reference/origin.md) and [terminology](terminology.md) explain these distinctions. Public synthetic runs show that selected computational routines execute; the empirical strength of a conclusion rests on the dissertation and its underlying evidence.
